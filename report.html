1. Comprehensive Problem Statement (5W1H Framework)

Who Faces the Problem: Healthcare professionals (general physicians, triage nurses, clinical specialists, radiologists, pharmacists) and patients navigating complex multi-facility healthcare provider networks.

What is the Problem: Critical patient clinical records are locked within isolated software silos and legacy paper folders. Clinicians cannot access historical vital trends, comprehensive surgical notes, allergy profiles, or concurrent medication regimens at the point of care, leading to frequent diagnostic rework, repeated laboratory investigations, medical charting errors, and uncoordinated care transitions.

When & Where Does It Occur: Continuously across inpatient hospital admissions, emergency room triage, outpatient clinic consultations, and community pharmacy dispensing counters.

Why is It Critical: Incomplete medical records cause life-threatening adverse drug events (such as administering contraindicated beta-lactam antibiotics to penicillin-allergic patients), delayed radiological assessments, diagnostic testing duplication, and reduced patient compliance due to lack of health record visibility.

How Will This Project Solve It: By designing, developing, and deploying a centralized, web-based Electronic Health Record System (EHRS01) featuring standardized Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE), structured SOAP clinical documentation with automated BMI monitoring, real-time Clinical Decision Support (CDS) allergy contraindication alerts, bidirectional HL7 v2.5.1 and FHIR R4 interoperability gateways, and an interactive self-service Patient Portal.

2. Proposed Solution & Approach

The developed solution is an enterprise-scale, full-stack Electronic Health Record System architected with modern React 19, Tailwind CSS, and a Node.js/Express service-oriented backend that complies with international healthcare data interchange standards. The platform eliminates clinical documentation fragmentation by implementing six fully functional clinical epics:

1. Master Patient Index & 360 View Centralized demographic records, insurance validation, emergency contacts, blood group, chronic conditions, and past surgeries.
2. SOAP Clinical Documentation Standardized Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan notes paired with biometric vital entry and automated dynamic BMI calculation.
3. CPOE Diagnostic Orders & PACS Laboratory and radiology ordering with priority triage (Routine, Urgent, STAT), LOINC standards, and a simulated DICOM Web viewer.
4. Medication Management & e-Rx Electronic prescribing with automated real-time allergy cross-reactivity warnings and documented clinical override safeguards.

To bridge heterogeneous hospital infrastructure, the system implements an Interoperability Hub supporting both legacy HL7 v2.5.1 pipe-delimited messages and modern HL7 FHIR Release 4 REST endpoints. Furthermore, a dual-role switcher allows switching seamlessly into the Patient Portal for self-service appointments and encrypted clinician messaging.

3. Detailed Elaboration of the 6 Agile Epics in Scope

The project was executed following an Agile Scrum framework structured across 20 distinct delivery sprints. Each epic targets critical operational workflows within modern healthcare institutions:

Epic 1: Patient Management & Master Patient Index (MPI) Planned: 3 Sprints | Status: Completed (100%)

Implements the Master Patient Index enabling patient search by full name, MRN (Medical Record Number), and email. Captures rich demographics (DOB, gender, address, emergency contact, blood group), verified insurance policy identifiers (Payer, Policy No, Group No, Expiration Date), chronic conditions (ICD-10 coded), past surgical histories, and documented drug allergies with severity grading. Generates standard HL7 ADT^A08 notification events upon demographic updates.

Epic 2: Clinical Documentation & 4-Quadrant SOAP Framework Planned: 4 Sprints | Status: Completed (100%)

Enables doctors and clinical staff to document consultations across outpatient, acute, and telehealth encounters. Includes biometric vitals capture: Blood Pressure (systolic/diastolic), Resting Heart Rate, Oxygen Saturation ($SpO_2$), Body Temperature, and Respiratory Rate. Features a dynamic Body Mass Index (BMI) engine calculating metric body mass index in real time. Features a structured 4-quadrant SOAP editor: Subjective (HPI), Objective (exam findings), Assessment (diagnoses & ICD-10), and Plan (orders & follow-ups).

Epic 3: Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE) & Diagnostic Review Planned: 3 Sprints | Status: Completed (100%)

Allows physicians to electronically order laboratory tests (CBC, CMP-14, HbA1c, Lipid Panel, Urinalysis) and imaging studies (Chest X-Ray, CT Brain, MRI Spine, Echocardiogram) tagged with standard LOINC codes. Supports priority classification (Routine, Urgent, STAT). Delivers verified laboratory result tables with reference intervals and automatic abnormal/critical flags. Integrates an interactive Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) DICOM Web viewer featuring zoom, contrast inversion, window leveling, and radiologist impressions.

Epic 4: Medication Management & E-Prescribing (e-Rx) with CDS Planned: 3 Sprints | Status: Completed (100%)

Provides an electronic prescribing workflow with RxNorm codes, dosage strengths, route of administration, frequency timing, authorized refills, and patient instructions. Features a built-in Clinical Decision Support (CDS) engine that actively checks for allergen contraindications and drug class cross-reactivity (e.g. flagging penicillin allergy when prescribing amoxicillin). Enforces mandatory clinician documentation if an emergency override is exercised. Tracks dispensing statuses via Surescripts integration.

Epic 5: Healthcare Interoperability & Integration Hub (HL7 & FHIR) Planned: 4 Sprints | Status: Completed (100%)

Bridges legacy and modern health IT systems by supporting both HL7 v2.5.1 ER7 pipe-delimited message generation/parsing (ADT^A01, ADT^A08, ORU^R01, RDE^O11) and modern HL7 FHIR Release 4 RESTful APIs (/api/fhir/Patient, /api/fhir/Observation, /api/fhir/MedicationRequest, /api/fhir/DiagnosticReport, and full patient $everything bundles). Maintains live simulated connectors to EMR, PACS, LIS, and Pharmacy systems with real-time latency monitoring.

Epic 6: Patient Portal & Secure Communication Planned: 3 Sprints | Status: Completed (100%)

Empowers patients with direct digital access to their health records. Includes a self-service appointment booking engine (physician selection, specialty, In-Person vs. Telehealth, time slots), encrypted two-way provider messaging threads, and transparent access to verified laboratory findings, active medications, and vital summaries.

Architectural Design Principles

Loose Coupling Modular service design allows independent evolution of clinical and interoperability engines without affecting frontend controllers.
Single-Process Serving Express hosts both the backend REST APIs and the pre-built React SPA from client/dist on port 5000, eliminating multi-server setup issues.
Standardized Vocabularies Enforces ICD-10 for diagnostic coding, LOINC for diagnostic lab observations, and RxNorm for pharmaceutical tracking.

9. Challenges Faced & Technical Resolutions

Challenge 1: Real-Time Drug-Allergy Cross-Reactivity Problem: Traditional prescribing software often fails to catch cross-reactive antibiotic classes (e.g., prescribing Amoxicillin to a patient documented as allergic to generic "Penicillin").
Resolution: Engineered a multi-tiered contraindication rule engine mapping drugs against exact allergens and pharmacological classes with an interactive clinical override modal requiring documented physician justification.
Challenge 2: Dual Legacy HL7 v2.5.1 & FHIR R4 Standards Problem: Hospitals rely on legacy pipe-delimited HL7 v2, while modern web apps require RESTful FHIR R4 JSON resources.
Resolution: Designed a modular transformation layer comprising an ER7 tokenizer/generator for standard HL7 segments (MSH, PID, PV1, OBR, OBX, RXE) and parallel JSON transformers exposing standard FHIR endpoints.
Challenge 3: Seamless Full-Stack Deployment Problem: Complex healthcare apps often require heavy external database setups and multiple terminals to run.
Resolution: Structured Express to host both REST APIs and the pre-built React SPA from client/dist on port 5000, supported by an automated Windows launcher (start.bat).
Challenge 4: Onsite-Offshore Sprint Coordination Problem: Managing handoffs across time zones between US onsite leadership and offshore engineers.
Resolution: Established a 4-hour daily overlap window, automated Jenkins CI/CD quality gates, and standardized Jira burndown metrics.
